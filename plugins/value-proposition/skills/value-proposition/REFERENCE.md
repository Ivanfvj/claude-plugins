# Value proposition: reference

## Bain "Elements of Value" (HBR, September 2016)

30 consumer elements in four tiers, the most powerful at the top.

- **Social impact:** self-transcendence
- **Life changing:** provides hope, self-actualization, motivation, heirloom, affiliation/belonging
- **Emotional:** reduces anxiety, rewards me, nostalgia, design/aesthetics, badge value, wellness, therapeutic value, fun/entertainment, attractiveness, provides access
- **Functional:** saves time, simplifies, makes money, reduces risk, organizes, integrates, connects, reduces effort, avoids hassles, reduces cost, quality, variety, sensory appeal, informs

Findings that shape step 4:

- Quality comes first in every industry: "no other elements can make up for a significant shortfall on this one."
- Companies scoring high on 4+ elements had about 3× the NPS and 4× the revenue growth of those with one. Apple scored high on 11 of 30: "Companies must choose their elements strategically."
- Companies strong on emotional elements average a higher NPS than those strong only on functional ones.
- Which elements matter after quality varies by industry; trailing on a crucial element comes before adding new ones.

## Quotes by source

**Copyhackers (Joanna Wiebe)**: definition and the five qualities.
- "A value proposition is a single statement that highlights what is unique and highly desirable about your offer."
- "Your value prop is written for the one offer you're trying to sell to one specific reader."
- Unique: "The part of your solution or the outcome of your solution that your competitors can't say or that they don't say."
- Desirable: "How much do your prospects want what's unique about your offering?"
- Specific: "something definite and graspable, such as an identifiable reference to your niche or something quantified."
- Succinct: "not as wordy as a mission statement, but it's not as short as one of those awful four-word tag lines."
- Sticky: "Your visitor will likely remember it – not recite it but recall it."
- "Write about 50 headlines before you put one on your home page." "Clear headlines trump clever headlines."

**April Dunford (Obviously Awesome)**: alternatives first, provable attributes.
- "So you start with competitive alternatives then you say what have I got that they don't have."
- "Only list attributes if you have concrete proof. Do not list opinions." (Commoncog summary)
- "'We save time' isn't a position; it's a placeholder." (2026 AMA)
- Anti-patterns: "mistake messaging for positioning, position the feature instead of the value."

**Strategyzer (Alexander Osterwalder)**: canvas and fit checklist.
- "Have you named a specific customer segment, not 'everyone'?"
- "Is the pain you relieve worth more to the customer than the price you charge?"
- "Have you checked whether the next best alternative (including doing nothing) is good enough?"
- "Is your differentiation visible to customers — not just to your competitors?"
- "Having a cool technology is not the same thing as having a value proposition."

**NN/g**: homepage clarity.
- "A well-defined value proposition should answer the question, 'Why should I choose this site/company over others?'"
- "People rarely read online — they're more likely to scan instead."
- "Use words that resonate with people and avoid jargon, business terminology, and feature-driven language."

**Unbounce**: clarity and the 5-second test.
- A generic headline got 6% correct answers to "What does the product do?" in a 5-second test; promoting the specific subhead to headline raised it to 20%.
- "your subhead is there to add Clarity, not be the sole holder of it."
